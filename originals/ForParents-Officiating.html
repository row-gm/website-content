Officiating

An essential role for our swim meets — no experience needed, and every hour counts toward your family’s FPP points.

Why Officiating Matters

Swim meets are the heart of competitive swimming — they’re the milestones where our swimmers test their training, build resilience, and gain confidence. These events are entirely run by an army of dedicated volunteers, most of whom are our wonderful parents.

Volunteering at meets not only ensures the events can happen but also gives you a chance to learn more about the sport and how to best support your swimmer. We welcome volunteers of all experience levels and will provide you with all the training you need.

Who Runs Our Meets

  • Meet Management is responsible for organizing and running ROW-hosted swim meets. Reach them at meetmanager@rowswimming.ca.
  • Officials Development & Administration, a sub-committee of Meet Management, ensures there’s an appropriately trained pool of officials available to run ROW-hosted meets. Reach them at officials@rowswimming.ca.

Key Volunteer Roles

Here are some of the most common positions you’ll see on the pool deck. For more details on any of these positions, visit the Swim Ontario Positions Page.

Introductory Positions

You do not need any prior experience to take on these roles.

Timekeeper

What you do: Operate a stopwatch and assist with the electronic timing system for a specific lane.

Why it matters: Your role is crucial for recording accurate swim times.

Safety Marshal

What you do: Ensure swimmer safety by following the Swimming Canada Competition Warmup Safety Procedures during the warm-up period. Reports to the Referee.

Why it matters: Helps ensure swimmers have a positive experience right at the start of the session and helps keep everyone safe.

Marshal

What you do: Assist the Admin Desk in keeping swimmers organized on deck.

Why it matters: Marshalling swimmers into their heats minimizes the risk of missing a race, and an organized, orderly experience getting to the blocks helps swimmers focus on their upcoming race.

Inspector of Turns

What you do: Observe swimmers at the start and during turns to ensure they’re following the rules for their specific stroke.

Why it matters: You help ensure fairness and consistency for all swimmers.

Senior Officials Positions

Senior officials mentor and supervise other volunteers, ensuring the entire meet runs smoothly and according to the rules. Officials must have experience on deck before being trained for these positions. Some, like Referee, require certification in multiple other roles first.

Judge of Stroke

What you do: Observe swimmers throughout the race to ensure they’re following the rules for their specific stroke.

Why it matters: Consistent, accurate stroke judging protects fairness for every swimmer in the race.

Starter

What you do: Working with the Referee, responsible for starting the race and judging the fairness of the start.

Why it matters: A fair, consistent start is the foundation of a fair race.

Admin Desk

What you do: Organizes swimmers on the deck and ensures all information is up to date.

Why it matters: Keeps the meet running on schedule and swimmers where they need to be.

Chief Timekeeper

What you do: In charge of the timekeepers, and provides assistance to them.

Why it matters: Supports accurate timing across every lane, every race.

Chief Judge Electronics

What you do: Supervises the inspectors of turns and judges of stroke, ensuring the rules are applied correctly.

Why it matters: Keeps stroke and turn judging consistent across the whole meet.

Referee

What you do: The highest authority at a meet, responsible for the overall operation and integrity of the competition.

Why it matters: Every decision on deck ultimately runs through the Referee.

Officials interested in training for senior positions should become familiar with the certification pathway and reach out to officials@rowswimming.ca so we can support your growth as an official.

Officiating Expectations & Requirements

We require the help of ROW families to run and participate in swim meets. Membership and participation in any swim club in Canada includes the expectation that families and swimmers will assist with running meets. These expectations include:

  • Registration as an official.
  • Attending necessary swimming officiating clinics.
  • Officiating at ROW meets if your swimmer is in the meet.

To Assist on Deck, Volunteers Must:

  • Be 14 years of age and older.
  • Register annually to become an Active official each swim season (September 1 – August 31).
  • Complete the training requirements for the position(s) they are working at a competition.

The club’s officials administrators are happy to guide new volunteers through the process. Reach out to officials@rowswimming.ca to get started.

Time spent helping run meets is eligible for Family Participation Program points.

How to Get Started as an Official

1

Register

Complete the registration form that’s shared with the ROW membership each swimming season. Re-registration is required each year. If you didn’t receive an email with a link to the most recent form, contact officials@rowswimming.ca.
2

Training

If it’s your first time, complete one or two quick online training courses provided by Swim Ontario. We’ll send you all the information you need.
3

Let Us Know You’re Available

Watch your inbox for volunteer opportunities, sent approximately four weeks before each meet. Registered officials are the first to be contacted for these roles.
4

On the Day of the Meet

Wear a red or white shirt and black pants, shorts, or a skirt. Your team will provide you with your assignment and any equipment you need.
5

Have Fun!

You’re now part of the team that makes every meet possible — enjoy it.
