ROW Swim Club

From Plan to Pool Deck

Our development philosophy is not a separate document from our program. Here is how each belief shows up in what actually happens at the pool.

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

Beliefs are EASY. Design choices are not.

Any club can write down that it develops the whole athlete based on sound human development principles. What matters is the timetable, the group structure, the training system, and what a coach is asked to do on deck.

Every program choice we make at ROW traces back to the best interest of the swimmer. If we learned something new that would help our swimmers, we would change the program.

We are building for progressive development

Most swimmers do not reach their true potential until they are well into their twenties. Early age group results are more often early maturation and birthday gap related. Our program design and structure develops for optimizing full potential.

What we believe
The ladder has to start somewhere a beginner can reach.
So we built
The ROW Swim Academy. The Foundation pathway, and the first rung. Saturday mornings at Cameron Heights, three class options of forty minutes each, with a coach on every lane. Many of those coaches are our own senior swimmers, on deck fifteen minutes after finishing their own session.
What we believe
Swimmers are developed as people first, and decisions are weighed against incremental steps that promote ongoing improvement, season after season.
So we built
A ladder with small steps. Sessions per week rise two, three, three then four through Junior. Four, five then six through Regional. Five, six then seven through Provincial. Eight at National. Nobody jumps from four sessions to eight.
The whole ladder, from two hours a week to fourteen. The gaps between rungs are deliberately small.
What we believe
The question we keep asking is how to get a swimmer to the age when they can fully realize their potential, still loving the sport.
So we built
A Pathway system. Swimmers have choices of programming that reflect their current mental and physical development, and does not limit their ability to change their choices as their lives change.
What we believe
Twelve hours of recovery between an evening session and the next morning.
So we built
A scheduling rule that cannot be broken. No group finishes in the evening and starts at 6:00 am. It is met everywhere in the timetable. Growing bodies need the sleep more than they need the extra session.

A birth year is not a training group

The Birthday Gap and The Growth Spurt both say the same thing: two swimmers the same age on paper can be years apart in development. A program that ignores that will misplace swimmers.

What we believe
Where a swimmer sits close to the top of an age band, we will be flexible.
So we built
Placement by readiness, not by birth year. The plan says it plainly: a swimmer wanting to pursue competition should be in a training group that suits them rather than one their birth year puts them in.
What we believe
Swimmers at different stages need different amounts, not different sports.
So we built
AGD split into two age-specific groups. One group of about seventy becomes AGD 12 & Under at four sessions a week and AGD 14 & Under at five, each with its own lead coach. JD is offered as JD1 and JD2, training alongside each other with different volumes.
This is why we publish written criteria for moving between groups and between pathways. A move should be explainable, and it should never come down to a single race result.

Times are one measure, not the measure

The ROW Way describes twelve things we develop across four areas: physical, technical, mental and character. A program that only measured times would be measuring one of the four.

What we believe
Progress is measured against all aspects of athlete and human development, not just a swimmer's times in their best events.
So we built
Two formal progress conversations a year, with the swimmer and the family, against objectives set at the start of the phase. Not a results printout.
What we believe
Families should be able to describe their swimmer's plan.
So we built
A written season plan for every group, setting out the phases, the technical priorities and the objectives, shared with families. Every group, including the two and three session programs.

Skills are taught, not hoped for

Starts, turns, underwaters and stroke mechanics do not depend on being big or being old. They are the things a swimmer can build while they wait for their growth spurt, and the things they keep afterwards.

What we believe
Technical work should be on a plan, not fitted in when time allows.
So we built
Technical work on a named cycle for every group, so skills are taught on a schedule rather than whenever a session runs short.
What we believe
A swimmer learns faster when they can see what they are being asked to do.
So we built
Underwater video on a regular cadence, with the footage shown to the swimmer, and a GoSwim account for every swimmer outside the Academy and every coach. Thousands of videos of age group and elite swimmers, so a skill can be seen before it is attempted.

The same set, a different prescription

Sprinters, middle distance and distance swimmers share a lane. They do not need entirely different sets. They need the same set with different numbers.

What we believe
Training should fit the swimmer.
So we built
Differentiated work within every session, so sprint, middle distance and distance swimmers in the same lane are not doing identical work.
What we believe
A coach can only individualize what they can see.
So we built
Swimmers per lane falls as the level rises. National trains four to a lane, Provincial 1 at five, and the Recreation groups at four or fewer. In our youngest program every single lane has its own coach.

We develop coaches the same way we develop swimmers

A club cannot promise long-term development to swimmers and treat its coaches as interchangeable.

Every coach has a development plan. Certification, time on deck with a mentor, and support to take on more as they are ready. Assistant roles are a route into the job, not a holding pattern.

Mentor coaches work across every group, at training and at meets, so what our most experienced coaches know is passed on while they are still here to pass it on.

ROW alumni coach here. Senior swimmers coach the Academy on Saturday mornings, straight after their own session.

That last one is The ROW Way working in both directions. A senior swimmer teaching an eight year old to streamline is learning something no set can teach them.

Common questions

Does a smaller program get a smaller version of this?
No. A written season plan, differentiated work, a technical cycle, underwater video, a GoSwim account, dryland with an owner and two progress conversations apply to every group, including the two and three session programs.
How do I know which group my swimmer should be in?
Written criteria for moving between groups and between pathways are published at the start of the season. Age band is a guide, not a rule, and readiness matters more than birth year.
Why not just train more?
Because more is not the goal; being able to keep going for years is. Session lengths are capped, recovery between sessions is protected, and only our two most senior groups ever train twice in a day.
Is the Recreation pathway a step down?
It is a different choice, not a lower one. It is for swimmers who want technique, fitness and a group without the competitive commitment. Staying in the sport is a good outcome.
Where can I read the full coaching framework?
The detailed framework is written for our coaching staff and lives in the coaches' area of the site. This page and the rest of this section cover what matters to swimmers and families.
