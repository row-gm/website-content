For Swimmers, Families and Coaches

The ROW Way

Our approach to using swimming to develop great human beings. Four things we develop, twelve things we watch for, and one scale that shows how far a swimmer has taken each of them.

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

What we are working towards

Our goal is to graduate athletes who are experts in themselves and their own performance. Whether they go on to university or college swimming, another sport, or none of it, they will know and be able to look after themselves.

That means every year a swimmer should need our direct coaching a little less. They should know what a hard set feels like, what their body needs afterwards, and what they are trying to fix. A swimmer who only performs when a coach is watching has not finished developing.

Swimming is how we do it, not why we do it. While a handful of ROW swimmers will make a career of this sport, all will carry what it taught them.

This page is not a checklist and it is not a ranking. It describes what we are developing and how we measure a swimmer's progress at each stage.

Four things we develop

They carry equal weight. A swimmer strong in one and absent in another is not further ahead.

Dimension The question What sits inside it
Physical What my body can do Endurance, speed, and looking after myself.
Technical What I can do in the water Skills, holding them under fatigue, and racing well.
Mental How I think and race Focus, bouncing back, and knowing what I am aiming at.
Character How I show up Being ready, being coachable, and being part of the team.

How far along: four steps

Every marker below moves through the same four steps. The steps are a handover. Each one moves a little more of the responsibility from the coach to the swimmer.

Step What it means Who is driving
Learning it Someone has shown me. I can do it with help. The coach
With reminders I can do it on my own when the coach reminds me. The coach, prompting
On my own I do it every practice without being told. The swimmer
When it's hard I do it tired, at speed, and when I race. The swimmer, under pressure
The final step is the real test of competency. A swimmer who only holds a skill when fresh has not finished learning it. Races, and most of life, are done when we are not at 100 percent capacity.

Twelve things we watch for

Written the way a swimmer would say them, because they are for the swimmer first.

Physical
1
I can keep going. I finish the session and hold the effort through a long set.
2
I can go fast. Every short sprint is full speed, not just the first one.
3
I look after myself. Sleep, food after practice, water, arriving ready.
Technical
4
I hold my streamline and underwaters off every wall.
5
I keep my stroke together when I get tired.
6
I can swim my race, not just swim fast. Starts, turns, finishes, pacing.
Mental
7
I know what I am working on today and why. I stay with it.
8
I bounce forward from a bad swim or a hard set. I learn and adapt.
9
I know what I am aiming at and what steps are required.
Character
10
I am ready to train. On time, kit packed, mindset focused.
11
I am coachable. I listen, I try the change, I ask when I do not understand.
12
I am part of the team. I cheer, I include people, I leave the deck better.

How to read this

1
For swimmers
Where you are now is not a grade and it is not a ranking. Find your stage, read down the column, and pick one thing to move up a step. One. Then tell your coach which one you picked.
2
For families
"With reminders" means your swimmer can do it, and still needs prompting. That is normal and it is not a complaint. The most useful thing you can do is ask what they are working on, and then leave the coaching to the coach.
3
For coaches
This is descriptive. Use it in conversation, in season reviews, and when explaining a group move. Do not turn it into a checklist a swimmer has to pass. If a marker does not fit your group, say so.

How we use it through the year

The model is not a document to read once. It is what our conversations about a swimmer are built on.

Where When What happens
Swimmer reviews Twice a season Coach and swimmer go through the twelve markers together. The swimmer says where they think they are first. They leave with one or two things to work on, not twelve.
Season planning Start of a cycle A group's weak markers shape what the coach emphasizes in the next block. If half a group cannot hold stroke when tired, that is a training decision, not a talk.
Group assessments When a move is being considered This is one input among several, alongside times, attendance, training history and readiness. It gives everyone the same language for the conversation. It is not a pass mark and no single marker decides a move.
Group moves are about readiness. They are a coaching judgement about when a swimmer has maximized the challenge and development of their current group. The details on this page make sure the conversation about that readiness uses the same words every time, in every group.
