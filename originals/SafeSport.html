A Shared Commitment

Safe sport at ROW isn’t a standalone club policy — it’s the local link in a chain that runs from the federal government through our national and provincial sport bodies, down to every practice and meet we run.

Government of Canada
Sets the national mandate through the Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport, and funds the independent Canadian Safe Sport Program.
 
Swimming Canada
Has adopted the Canadian Safe Sport Program nationally and states plainly that every athlete, coach, official, and volunteer has the right to a training and competitive environment free of abuse, harassment, or discrimination.
 
Swim Ontario
Carries that mandate provincially, committed to fun, healthy, inclusive, and safe environments built on fairness, integrity, open communication, and mutual respect.
 
ROW Swim Club
We’re the local stakeholders. We create the day-to-day environments aligned with that commitment, and we protect our members from anyone or anything that isn’t.

Reporting a Concern

If you require assistance with filing a complaint, please contact Swim Ontario through their Club Resources page.

For additional information, review Swim Ontario’s Sport Safety policies, including the procedures for Complaints, Dispute Resolution, Discipline, and Appeals.

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
3

Rowan’s Law (Concussion Awareness)

The Rowan’s Law Regulations came into effect on July 1, 2019 in Ontario and are mandatory for all sports organizations.

As part of registration, all swimmers and families under the age of 26, and all coaches and officials Level 3 and higher, must have read and agreed to the Rowan’s Law resources and declaration.

More information on Rowan’s Law is available on the Government of Ontario’s website, and on Swim Ontario’s Concussion Safety page.
