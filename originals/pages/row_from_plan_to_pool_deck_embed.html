Our Development Plan

From Plan to Pool Deck

Our philosophy is not a separate document from our program. Here is how each belief shows up in what actually happens at the pool.

Beliefs are easy. Design choices are not.

Any club can write down that it develops the whole athlete. What matters is the timetable, the group structure, the training system, and what each coach is asked to do.

Every program choice at ROW traces back to the best interest of the swimmer. If we learned something that would help our swimmers, we would change the program.

The whole ladder

From forty minutes on a Saturday to fourteen hours a week. The gaps between rungs are deliberately small: nobody jumps from four sessions to eight.

We are building for progressive development

What we believeSo we built
What we believeThe ladder has to start somewhere a beginner can reach.So we builtThe ROW Swim Academy. The first rung. Saturday mornings at Cameron Heights, three class options of forty minutes, with a coach on every lane. Many of those coaches are our own SD swimmers, on deck fifteen minutes after finishing their own session.
What we believeSwimmers are developed as people first, in steps that promote improvement season after season.So we builtA ladder with small steps. Sessions a week rise two, three, three then four through Junior. Four, five then six through Regional. Five, six then seven through Provincial. Eight at National. Nobody jumps from four sessions to eight.
What we believeSwimmers should be able to change their choice as their lives change.So we builtA pathway system. Six pathways with real choices, so a swimmer can move without leaving the sport.
What we believeTwelve hours of recovery between an evening session and the next morning.So we builtA scheduling rule that cannot be broken. No group finishes in the evening and starts at 6:00 am. It is met everywhere in the timetable. Growing bodies need the sleep more than they need the extra session.

A birth year is not a training group

What we believeSo we built
What we believeWhere a swimmer sits close to the top of an age band, we will be flexible.So we builtPlacement by readiness, not birth year. A swimmer wanting to compete should be in the group that suits them rather than the one their birth year puts them in.
What we believeSwimmers at different stages need different amounts, not different sports.So we builtAge Group Development split in two. One group of about seventy became AGD 2 at four sessions and AGD 1 at five, each with its own lead coach. Junior Development runs as JD1 and JD2, training alongside each other at different volumes.

Times are one measure, not the measure

What we believeSo we built
What we believeProgress is measured against every part of development, not just times in best events.So we builtTwo formal progress conversations a year, with the swimmer and the family, against objectives set at the start of the phase. Not a results printout.
What we believeFamilies should be able to describe their swimmer’s plan.So we builtA written season plan for every group, setting out the phases, the technical priorities and the objectives, shared with families. Every group, including the two and three session programs.

Skills are taught, not hoped for

What we believeSo we built
What we believeTechnical work should be on a plan, not fitted in when time allows.So we builtTechnical work on a named cycle for every group, so skills are taught on a schedule rather than whenever a session runs short.
What we believeA swimmer learns faster when they can see what they are being asked to do.So we builtUnderwater video on a regular cadence, with the footage shown to the swimmer, and a GoSwim account for every swimmer outside the Academy and every coach.

The same set, a different prescription

What we believeSo we built
What we believeTraining should fit the swimmer.So we builtDifferentiated work within every session, so sprint, middle distance and distance swimmers in the same lane are not doing identical work.
What we believeA coach can only individualize what they can see.So we builtSwimmers per lane falls as the level rises. National trains four to a lane, PD1 at five, and the Recreation groups at four or fewer. In our youngest program every lane has its own coach.

We develop coaches the way we develop swimmers

What we believeSo we built
What we believeA club cannot promise long-term development to swimmers and treat its coaches as interchangeable.So we builtEvery coach has a development plan. Certification, time on deck with a mentor, and support to take on more as they are ready. Assistant roles are a route into the job, not a holding pattern.
What we believeWhat our most experienced coaches know has to be passed on while they are here to pass it on.So we builtMentor coaches work across every group, at training and at meets. And ROW alumni coach here: SD swimmers take the Academy on Saturday mornings, straight after their own session.

Common questions

Does a smaller program get a smaller version of this?No. A written season plan, differentiated work, a technical cycle, underwater video, a GoSwim account, dryland with an owner and two progress conversations apply to every group, including the two and three session programs.
How do I know which group my swimmer should be in?Written criteria for moving between groups and pathways are published at the start of the season. Age band is a guide, and readiness matters more than birth year.
Why not just train more?Because more is not the goal; being able to keep going for years is. Session lengths are capped, recovery between sessions is protected, and only PD1 and ND ever train twice in a day.
Is the Recreation pathway a step down?It is a different choice, not a lower one. It is for swimmers who want technique, fitness and a group without the competitive commitment. Staying in the sport is a good outcome.
