For swimmers, families and coaches

The ROW Way

Our approach to using swimming to develop great human beings. Four things we develop, twelve things we watch for, and one scale showing how far a swimmer has taken each.

What we are working towards

Our goal is to graduate athletes who are experts in themselves and their own performance. Whether they go on to university swimming, another sport, or none of it, they will know how to look after themselves.

That means every year a swimmer should need our direct coaching a little less. They should know what a hard set feels like, what their body needs afterwards, and what they are trying to fix.

Swimming is how we do it, not why we do it. A handful of ROW swimmers will make a career of this sport; all of them will carry what it taught them.

This page is not a checklist and it is not a ranking. It describes what we are developing, and how we talk about a swimmer’s progress at each stage.

Four things we develop

They carry equal weight. A swimmer strong in one and absent in another is not further ahead.

AreaThe questionWhat sits inside it
AreaPhysicalThe questionWhat my body can doWhat sits inside itEndurance, speed, strength, and the ability to repeat hard work. The engine.
AreaTechnicalThe questionWhat I can do in the waterWhat sits inside itStroke, starts, turns, underwaters and pacing. The skills that hold up when you are tired.
AreaMentalThe questionHow I think and raceWhat sits inside itFocus, resilience, handling pressure and racing your own race. Trained on deck, not taught in a speech.
AreaCharacterThe questionHow I show upWhat sits inside itEffort, reliability, honesty, and how you treat the people around you. The part that outlasts the swimming.

How far along: four steps

Every marker moves through the same four steps. The steps are a handover: each one moves a little more of the responsibility from the coach to the swimmer.

StepWhat it meansWho is driving
StepLearning itWhat it meansSomeone has shown me. I can do it with help.Who is drivingThe coach
StepWith remindersWhat it meansI can do it on my own when the coach reminds me.Who is drivingThe coach, prompting
StepOn my ownWhat it meansI do it every practice without being told.Who is drivingThe swimmer
StepWhen it’s hardWhat it meansI do it tired, at speed, and when I race.Who is drivingThe swimmer, under pressure
The last step is the real test. A swimmer who only holds a skill when fresh has not finished learning it. Races, and most of life, happen when we are not at our best.

Twelve things we watch for

Written the way a swimmer would say them, because they are for the swimmer first.

Physical
I can keep going. I finish the session and hold the effort through a long set.
I can go fast. Every short sprint is full speed, not just the first one.
I look after myself. Sleep, food after practice, water, arriving ready.
Technical
I hold my streamline and underwaters off every wall.
I keep my stroke together when I get tired.
I can swim my race, not just swim fast. Starts, turns, finishes, pacing.
Mental
I know what I am working on today and why. I stay with it.
I bounce forward from a bad swim or a hard set. I learn and adapt.
I know what I am aiming at and what steps are required.
Character
I am ready to train. On time, kit packed, mindset focused.
I am coachable. I listen, I try the change, I ask when I do not understand.
I am part of the team. I cheer, I include people, I leave the deck better.

How to read this

1
For swimmers. Where you are now is not a grade and not a ranking. Find your stage, read down the column, and pick one thing to move up a step. One. Then tell your coach which one you picked.
2
For families. “With reminders” means your swimmer can do it and still needs prompting. That is normal and it is not a complaint. The most useful thing you can do is ask what they are working on, then leave the coaching to the coach.
3
For coaches. This is descriptive. Use it in conversation, in season reviews, and when explaining a group move. Do not turn it into a checklist a swimmer has to pass. If a marker does not fit your group, say so.

How we use it through the year

The model is not a document to read once. It is what our conversations about a swimmer are built on.

WhereWhenWhat happens
WhereSwimmer reviewsWhenTwice a seasonWhat happensCoach and swimmer go through the twelve markers together. The swimmer says where they think they are first. They leave with one or two things to work on, not twelve.
WhereSeason planningWhenStart of a cycleWhat happensA group’s weak markers shape what the coach emphasizes in the next block. If half a group cannot hold stroke when tired, that is a training decision, not a talk.
WhereGroup assessmentsWhenWhen a move is being consideredWhat happensOne input among several, alongside times, attendance, training history and readiness. It is not a pass mark, and no single marker decides a move.

Group moves are about readiness — a coaching judgement about when a swimmer has got everything they can from their current group. This page makes sure that conversation uses the same words every time.
