The Training Year

Why training looks the way it does, and why it changes as the year goes on.

Training is not the same all year

If you watched one practice a month for a season, you would see very different things. That is deliberate. Training moves through phases, each with a different job, and your swimmer's coach chooses which one the group is in.

Every ROW coach uses the same six names for these, and so does this page. When your swimmer says the group is in Accumulation, this is what they mean.

The six phases

What each one is for, and what you will notice at home.

PhaseHow longWhat it looks like
PhaseIntroductionENTRYHow long2 to 4 weeks, after a summer or a month awayWhat it looks likeBack into the habit. Technique, skills, and gentle swimming while the body remembers what it is doing.
PhaseAccumulationLOADHow longThe longest stretch of the yearWhat it looks likeThe heavy weeks. A lot of swimming at a steady effort. Race times often get slower here, and that is the plan working, not failing.
PhaseConsolidationUNLOADHow long4 to 7 days, after every hard stretchWhat it looks likeA lighter week so the last block of work can sink in. Swimmers improve while recovering from hard training, not during it.
PhaseIntensificationLOADHow long3 to 5 weeks before a big meetWhat it looks likeLess swimming, faster swimming. Race speed, starts, turns and race plans. Your swimmer will be tired in a different way.
PhasePeakingUNLOADHow long1 to 3 weeks, twice a yearWhat it looks likeThe taper. Training drops right down so the racing can come up. Only for the two meets a year the season is built around.
PhaseTransitionUNLOADHow long1 to 2 weeks at the end of a cycleWhat it looks likeRest, other sports, and a proper look back at the season. Scheduled on purpose, not whatever time is left over.
Slower times during a building phase are normal. Swimmers are training through fatigue on purpose. The speed shows up after the lighter weeks that follow, not during the heavy ones.

Effort, not speed

Coaches ask for an effort rather than a time. That is what lets one set work for a whole lane of different swimmers at once. A faster swimmer and a slower swimmer can be doing exactly the same training, and both be right.

Swimmers talk about zones at home, so here is what they mean.

ZoneWhat it feels like
ZoneZone 1What it feels likeVery easy. Could chat the whole way.

Your swimmer's coach decides which zones a group uses and how much. Younger swimmers spend almost all their time in the easier ones, and that is on purpose.

Hard days and easy days

A good week is not a week of hard sessions. It has hard days, easy days and at least one day off, in a deliberate order.

The easy session is not a wasted one. It is the session that lets the hard ones work. If your swimmer says practice was easy today, that is usually the plan rather than a coach going easy on them.

One thing that surprises people. Swimmers in the same lane, on the same set, are often given different intervals, distances or targets. They are doing the same training, fitted to them. Nobody is being given less.
