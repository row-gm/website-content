ROW Swim Club

Code of Conduct

ROW Swim Club is committed to a safe, respectful environment for every swimmer, parent, coach, volunteer, and official connected to our club.

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

Our Commitment

Everyone who is part of ROW Swim Club is entitled to an environment free from bullying, harassment, violence, and discrimination. This code sets out what we expect from all members and explains what to do if those expectations are not met.

ROW is a member of Swim Ontario and Swimming Canada. This code is written to be consistent with the Swim Ontario Code of Conduct (O-002-06) and the Swimming Canada Code of Conduct (BD-HR-02). In any conflict between this code and those documents, the governing body codes take precedence.

There are no repercussions for anyone who raises a concern in good faith — even if the concern turns out to be a misunderstanding.

Who This Applies To

This code applies to all ROW members and to anyone acting on behalf of the club, including:

Swimmers
All registered ROW swimmers, at all club activities.
Parents and guardians
All parents and guardians of registered swimmers — at practices, meets, club events, and online.
Coaches and staff
All ROW coaches, administrators, and paid staff.
Volunteers
Parent volunteers, event helpers, board directors, and all others acting on behalf of the club.
Officials and guests
Visiting officials and guests participating in ROW-hosted events.

This code applies during practices, swim meets, training camps, club meetings, travel associated with club activities, and in online spaces connected to ROW.

What We Expect

Respect
Treat all people with dignity. This includes swimmers, coaches, parents, officials, and members of other clubs. Disrespectful language, gestures, or behaviour toward any person is not acceptable.
Inclusion
ROW does not tolerate discrimination of any kind — based on race, ancestry, ethnicity, language, religion, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, or any other ground. Every member belongs here.
Honesty and integrity
Be honest in all dealings with the club. Do not misrepresent a swimmer's age, eligibility, or registration status.
Fair play
Compete and support competition with integrity. Respect the results and the efforts of all athletes.
Confidentiality
Respect the privacy of other members. Do not share personal information about other swimmers or families without permission.

ROW-Specific Expectations

Pool deck

Only coaches and authorized staff are permitted on the pool deck during practice. Parents and guardians observe from the designated viewing area. This boundary protects the coaching relationship and keeps the deck safe for swimmers.

Swim meets

How we behave at meets reflects on every swimmer in our club. Cheer for your swimmer and for others. Do not coach from the stands — your swimmer has a coach. Direct concerns about meet conduct to the designated ROW staff member, not to officials directly.

Communicating with coaches

Coaches are focused on your swimmer during practice. Questions about training, progress, or scheduling should go to office@rowswimming.ca or through your group's parent representative. Approaching a coach during practice is appropriate only for urgent safety concerns. If you disagree with a coaching decision, the right channel is email or a scheduled meeting — not the pool deck.

Online and social media

Online conduct is subject to this code. Do not post photos or videos that identify other swimmers without their family's consent. Do not post content that is harmful, embarrassing, or defamatory toward any ROW member, coach, or official — including in private or closed online groups.

Unacceptable Conduct

The following types of conduct are not acceptable at ROW. They may result in suspension or removal from the club, and may be referred to Swim Ontario or other authorities.

Bullying
Unwelcome or unreasonable behaviour that demeans, intimidates, or humiliates a person — individually or as a group. Bullying is often part of a pattern, but a single serious incident also qualifies.

Examples: abusive language, insults, teasing, spreading rumours, unreasonable criticism, deliberate exclusion, repeatedly belittling someone's ideas or work.
Cyberbullying
Using technology to carry out deliberate, hostile behaviour intended to harm another person. This includes messaging apps, social media, gaming chat, and email.

Examples: sending threatening or humiliating messages, spreading rumours online, posting damaging content about someone, impersonating someone to cause harm, creating content that portrays a person negatively.
Harassment
A course of comment or conduct directed at a person that is known — or ought reasonably to be known — to be unwelcome. Harassment is a form of discrimination under Ontario's Human Rights Code and can be an offence under Canada's Criminal Code.

Examples: unwelcome remarks about a person's identity, appearance, or beliefs; persistent unwanted contact; condescending or patronizing behaviour intended to undermine someone; practical jokes that cause embarrassment or endanger safety.
Sexual harassment
Unwelcome conduct or comment related to a person's sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, or gender expression. A single incident can constitute sexual harassment.

Examples: sexual advances or requests; sexually explicit language or jokes; inappropriate physical contact; displaying or sharing offensive sexual material.
Violence
A threat, attempt, or act of physical force that causes or could cause physical injury.

Examples: hitting, pushing, kicking, or shoving; threatening behaviour interpreted as intent to cause harm; disruptive behaviour that endangers others (e.g., aggressive yelling or throwing objects).
Mobbing
A form of group bullying where a person is targeted through collective humiliation, general harassment, or emotional abuse. Individual members of the group may carry out specific acts — an insult, a practical joke — as part of the pattern.

Responsibilities

Every member has a role in keeping ROW safe.

Board of Directors
 
Know this policy and ensure all coaches, parents, and swimmers are aware of it.
 
Ensure any incident is handled according to this policy.
 
Investigate and respond promptly, sensitively, and confidentially to confirmed incidents.
 
Seek guidance from Swim Ontario's Safe Sport office when needed.
Coaches and Staff
 
Know and follow this policy in all club activities.
 
Report and document incidents witnessed or brought to their attention to the Board.
Parents and Guardians
 
Know and follow this policy.
 
Report incidents involving your child, or that you have witnessed, to a Board member.
 
Encourage your swimmer to do the same.
Swimmers
 
Know and follow this policy.
 
Report incidents that happen to you or that you witness to your parents, a coach, or a Board member.

Safe Sport

ROW is committed to the Swim Ontario Safe Sport principles. Physical, psychological, and sexual maltreatment have no place in our club.

If your concern involves abuse, harassment, grooming, or any conduct under the UCCMS (Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport), you may contact Swim Ontario directly. You do not need to go through ROW first, and there are no consequences for doing so.

Swim Ontario Safe Sport: swimontario.com/safe-sport

If there is immediate risk to a child or anyone else, contact local police or child protective services.

Raising a Concern

ROW has a structured process for members to raise feedback or concerns. Whether you are frustrated and need to be heard, or want a formal response, the Member Feedback Form is the starting point.

Use the Member Feedback Form to submit your concern. The form walks you through the relevant policies and lets you tell us what you would like to happen next. All submissions are reviewed by club leadership. You will receive an acknowledgement within two business days.

Group Parent Representatives have a direct channel to the Board for group-level feedback. If you are a designated Group Parent Rep, use that channel for concerns on behalf of your group.
