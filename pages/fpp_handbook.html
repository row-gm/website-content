Running the FPP tool

You look after the Family Partnership Program. This page tells you how, in the order you will need it. The three weekly steps are at the top. Everything below explains what the words mean, and is there for the day something does not look right.

Every week

Two menu clicks and one look. Ten minutes at most.

  1. 1 FPP › 1. Run Activity Update

    Brings in what families have submitted, checks every entry, and rebuilds the totals. It takes about a minute and a half. Wait for it.

  2. 2 Open the Activity Log and read anything in red

    Red means Needs fixing. The reason is written in the Checked column beside it. Put the cause right. Step 1 will look at it again next time and clear it by itself.

  3. 3 FPP › 2. Approve All Waiting

    Nothing counts towards a family's total until you do this. That is on purpose.

If you only remember one thing

Run step 1, fix what is red, run step 3. You cannot break anything by running step 1 twice. It skips whatever it has already dealt with.

The other jobs

These come up now and then, not weekly.

After a meet

Two steps, in this order.

  • FPP › Setup › Check Meet Activity — a look ahead. Nothing is brought in and nothing is changed. It fills the Meet Check tab with anything that needs sorting out, and gives you the row number on the meet team's own sheet so you can point them straight at it.
  • FPP › Import Meet Activity — brings it all in. Then run step 1 above.

Every row that is not already on the Activity Log comes in, including the ones with something wrong. That is deliberate. A row nobody can ever fix — a visiting official, a family who has left — gets set to Declined once, and is never raised again.

When the roster changes

Paste the new export onto the Roster Import tab, then FPP › Import New Roster. It rebuilds the totals by itself, so you do not need to run anything else.

Activity you are recording yourself

Board roles, casino, bingo, fundraising — things a family should not be submitting on the form. Paste them onto Batch Add Activity, then FPP › Add a Batch of Activity.

It is all or nothing. If any row has a problem, nothing is brought in and the reason appears in the Problem column. Your batch stays where it is, so you can fix it and try again.

New activities on the family form

FPP › Setup › Match Activities To Form reads your form and adds anything the Activities tab does not know about, ready for you to price. FPP › Setup › Match Activities To Meet List does the same job from the meet team's Positions tab.

Neither one renames anything or switches anything off. A reworded activity and a brand new one look identical from the tool's side, and renaming the wrong row would move points off the activity they belong to. It reports those instead, for you to settle.

What the tool actually is

Read this once. You will not need it again.

Families owe a number of volunteer points each season. How many depends on their swimmer's group. The tool works out what each family owes, collects what they have done, checks it, and adds it up.

It is one Google Sheet with an FPP menu added to it. Everything happens from that menu. There is also a read-only web page you can share, so people can see where they stand without being given the workbook.

Three things feed it:

  • A Google Form that families fill in themselves.
  • The meet team's workbook, which the tool reads and never writes to.
  • You, pasting in batches for anything the first two do not cover.

Nothing that arrives counts straight away. Every entry is checked, then waits for a person to approve it.

The five words on the Activity Log

The Status column is the whole story. Everything else follows from it.

StatusWhat it meansWhat you do
New Just arrived. Not looked at yet. Nothing. Run Activity Update will pick it up.
Needs fixing Something is wrong. The reason is in the Checked column. Put the cause right. The next Run Activity Update rechecks it.
Waiting Passed every check. Ready for a person to say yes. Approve All Waiting.
Counted Approved. It is in the family's total. Nothing. Done.
Declined Turned down, or never fixable. Nothing. It is closed for good and will not be raised again.
Why Declined matters

Setting a row to Declined is how you stop something coming back. The Activity Log is the tool's memory. Once a row is on it, no import will ever raise that same entry again.

What the red messages mean

These are the exact words you will see in the Checked column, and what to do about each.

“someone@example.com” does not match any family. Add it to Extra Emails, or correct it here.
A parent used an address the club does not have on file. Put it on the Extra Emails tab, pointing at their real account address. You only do this once per address, ever.
No email address.
The entry has nobody attached to it. Type the family's address into the Submitted Email column.
“Timer” is not on the Activities tab.
Somebody reported something the club has not priced. Add it to the Activities tab and give it a value, or correct the spelling on the log.
“Timer” is switched off this season.
The activity exists but its Active column says No. Switch it back on, or decline the entry.
“Casino” is worth a different amount every time, so it needs a number.
The activity is priced as Variable. Type what this one is worth into the Points Override column.
That date is outside the season.
The date falls before Season Starts or after Season Ends on the Settings tab. Either the date is wrong, or it really was last season and should be declined.
That date is text, not a date.
The cell looks like a date but the sheet is treating it as words. Format that column as a date and retype it.
No date. Put the day, or say when it was in words.
Blank. A plain description works fine here — “the fall session” is accepted. Only a real date is checked against the season.
This looks like the same entry twice.
The same family, activity and date is already on the log. If it genuinely happened twice, change the Details so the two are not identical.

The Settings tab

Fourteen rows. Three columns. Only the middle one is yours.

  • Column B, the yellow Value column, is safe to change. That is what the tab is for. Nothing you run will overwrite it.
  • Column A, the names on the left, should be left alone. The tool finds each setting by its exact name. A renamed one reads as zero rather than raising an error.
  • Column C explains each one and is rewritten every time the workbook is set up, so there is no point editing it.

You can add your own rows anywhere. They will survive, but the tool will not read them — an extra row is a note to yourself unless somebody wires it in.

The three that matter most

First Year %, First Year Least and Activity Cap % are called out by Update Totals if they ever go blank. The others are not, so a blank Carry Forward % simply means nobody gets credit, and nothing says so.

Anywhere a workbook is asked for — Review Workbook ID, Meet Source ID — you can paste the whole web address. You do not have to dig the long code out of the middle of it.

When something looks wrong

A family's total is zero and should not be

Check their entries are Counted, not still Waiting. If they are Counted, run FPP › Update Totals.

A whole column has gone blank

Somebody deleted a row. See the rule at the bottom of this page. Running FPP › Setup › Set Up Workbook puts the formulas back.

The menu is missing

Reload the page. The FPP menu is added when the workbook opens, and it takes a second or two to appear.

Nothing came in from a meet

Run Check Meet Activity and read the Meet Check tab. The most common cause is that the meet team's sheet is still showing last season.

A script runs for a long time

That is normal. Run Activity Update takes about a minute and a half, and Set Up Workbook about seventy seconds. Let it finish rather than clicking again.

Links

Hold Ctrl as you click — ⌘ on a Mac — and the link opens in a new tab, so the handbook stays where it is.

The report page

Open it with your club account and it lets you in — there is no key to remember, nothing to send round and nothing to leak. Google tells the page who you are, and only a rowswimming.ca account is recognised. Your address is shown at the top so you can see which account you are using.

For anyone without a club account, the old style of link still works: add ?key= and the value in the Report Key row of Settings. Change that value and every keyed link stops working at once.

The one rule that matters

Never delete rows

Not on Activity Log, Roster, Standings or Last Season.

Every calculated column on those tabs is a single formula sitting in row 2, filling the whole column below it. Delete row 2 and that formula is gone. The column does not show an error — it just quietly stops working, and totals start coming out at zero.

To clear something, select the cells and clear the contents. Leave the rows where they are.
