How placements are decided

Placement is about readiness. A coach is asking one question: which group fits this swimmer right now?

To answer it, coaches look at several things together.

  • How the swimmer is progressing across the twelve things we watch for
  • Times and race results
  • Attendance and training history
  • Whether the swimmer is ready for the day-to-day demands of the group

No single one of these decides a move. It is a coaching judgement, not a test to pass.

What we watch for

We watch twelve things across four areas. They carry equal weight. A swimmer strong in one and absent in another is not further ahead. They are written the way a swimmer would say them, because they are for the swimmer first.

Area In a swimmer's own words
Physical
  • I can keep going. I finish the session and hold the effort through a long set.
  • I can go fast. Every short sprint is full speed, not just the first one.
  • I look after myself. Sleep, food after practice, water, arriving ready.
Technical
  • I hold my streamline and underwaters off every wall.
  • I keep my stroke together when I get tired.
  • I can swim my race, not just swim fast. Starts, turns, finishes, pacing.
Mental
  • I know what I am working on today and why. I stay with it.
  • I bounce forward from a bad swim or a hard set. I learn and adapt.
  • I know what I am aiming at and what steps are required.
Character
  • I am ready to train. On time, kit packed, mindset focused.
  • I am coachable. I listen, I try the change, I ask when I do not understand.
  • I am part of the team. I cheer, I include people, I leave the deck better.

The four steps

Each of the twelve moves through the same four steps. Each step hands a little more of the responsibility from the coach to the swimmer.

1

Learning it

Someone has shown me, and I can do it with help.
2

With reminders

I can do it on my own when the coach reminds me.
3

On my own

I do it every practice without being told.
4

When it's hard

I do it tired, at speed, and when I race.

Common questions

When will we know our swimmer's group?
Suggested group assignments go out in August. Registration opens in September.
Do we need to do anything when the notice arrives?
No. There is nothing to pay or fill in until registration opens in September. The notice is there so you know which group to plan for.
Our swimmer's friends are in another group. Can they move to be with them?
We place swimmers by readiness rather than by friend groups. We know friendships matter, and we do not take that lightly. But a swimmer in the wrong group for their stage rarely enjoys it for long. The good news is that friendships at ROW are not limited to one group. Meets, camps and club events bring swimmers together across the whole club.
We think our swimmer is ready for a higher group. What should we do?
Tell us. Start with your swimmer's coach, who sees them in the water every week. Bring what you are seeing at home, and what your swimmer is saying about their own goals. That is useful information, and coaches want it. The placement decision stays with the coaching staff, but it is a better decision when we hear from you.
Can a swimmer change groups during the season?
Yes. Most of our swimmer movement happens at two points in the year: January, based on progress from September through December, and April, based on progress from January through March. So a placement in September is not a decision that holds until next fall. If a swimmer is ready in January, that is when it happens. We speak with the family before any change.
A swimmer is stretched too far. What then?
Sometimes a group turns out to be too much, and that is worth saying out loud. A swimmer stretched a little grows. A swimmer stretched too far often loses the joy in it. Protecting that matters more to us than moving a swimmer up quickly. Moves in both directions are normal, and neither is a failure.
How can we help at home?
Two things help most. Steady attendance: progress comes from showing up, week after week. Ask what they are working on: then leave the coaching to the coach. If your swimmer picks one thing they want to move up a step and tells their coach, that is exactly how this is meant to work.
Questions about your swimmer's placement? Speak with your swimmer's coach first. For anything else, email the club office at office@rowswimming.ca.
