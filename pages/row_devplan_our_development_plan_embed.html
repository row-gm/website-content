Our Development Plan

How we use swimming to build fast swimmers and capable young adults. Written for swimmers, coaches and families, in the same words.

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

What we are trying to do

Our goal is to graduate swimmers who are experts in themselves and their own performance. Swimmers who know how they train, how they think, how they race, why it works, and what to do when it does not.

Only a handful of our swimmers will make a career of this sport. All of them will carry what it taught them. So we train the swimmer and the person at the same time, on purpose rather than by accident.

Swimming is the vehicle, not the point. A swim club is a good place to learn things that are hard to teach anywhere else: how to work at something for years, how to lose and come back, how to be honest about your own performance, how to be part of a group that depends on you. This is not a softer version of competing. The swimmers who develop these habits are the ones who end up fastest.

The four things we develop

Everything we coach falls into one of four areas. A swimmer is not finished when they are fit. They are finished when all four are in place.

Area The question What sits inside it
AreaPhysical The questionWhat my body can do What sits inside itEndurance, speed, strength, and the ability to repeat hard work. The engine.
AreaTechnical The questionWhat I can do in the water What sits inside itStroke, starts, turns, underwaters and pacing. The skills that hold up when you are tired.
AreaMental The questionHow I think and race What sits inside itFocus, resilience, handling pressure and racing your own race. Trained on deck, not taught in a speech.
AreaCharacter The questionHow I show up What sits inside itEffort, reliability, honesty, and how you treat the people around you. The part that outlasts the swimming.

A system, not a collection of sessions

Behind every practice is a plan that runs from the season down to a single set. Coaches share one vocabulary, so a swimmer moving between groups is not starting over.

1
The season is organized in phases. Blocks of training with different jobs: building, sharpening, recovering, racing. Each one is chosen, not inherited from last year.
2
The week has a shape. Hard days and easy days sit in a deliberate order, and load builds across weeks rather than randomly.
3
Every practice is built the same way. So the same set means the same thing on every deck, in every group.
4
The prescription fits the swimmer. Same set, same purpose, different interval, distance or target.

The full coaching framework is written for our coaching staff and lives in the coaches’ area. What matters to a swimmer or a family is that it exists, that it is written down, and that every coach works from the same one.

Common questions

   
Is this section for swimmers or for parents? Both, and for coaches too. These pages are deliberately written once, in words everyone can use, so a swimmer and a parent are reading the same thing.
Where is the detailed coaching framework? In the coaches’ area of the site. It covers training phases, zones, week structure and session design. It is written for staff and needs coach access.
Does this replace talking to my coach? No. It gives you the shared language for a better conversation. Your coach knows your swimmer; a web page does not.
My swimmer is young. Does any of this apply yet? Yes, in a lighter form. All four areas start on day one. What changes as a swimmer grows is how much of each, and how much they run themselves.
