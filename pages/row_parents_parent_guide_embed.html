New Parent Essentials

Welcome to ROW! This page is a starting point for both new and returning families — a short orientation if you're just getting started, and a reference for specific questions once you're settled in.

Five Steps to Get Started

1

Assessment with Our Coaches

No matter what level your swimmer is at, all new athletes must complete an assessment with one of our coaches. Assessments are typically scheduled in early September, but we also offer them throughout the season depending on demand. If your swimmer has experience with another club, the coach may place them directly into the appropriate group.
2

Registering for Your Assigned Group

After the assessment, the coach will recommend the most suitable group for your swimmer. You’ll then complete registration for that specific group based on the coach’s recommendation.
3

Getting the Right Equipment

Each group requires swimmers to have specific training equipment. Visit the ROW Equipment List for details on what your swimmer will need.
4

Checking Your Group’s Start Date

The start date for each training group is posted on that group’s program page. Be sure to review your group’s page so your swimmer is ready to start on time.
5

Payment Information

In mid-September, you’ll receive an email with instructions on payment options and the due date for your first installment. You must reply to this email to confirm your choice of payment method. Season fees are posted each August on the Registration page at rowswimming.ca.
All Set! Once you’ve completed your assessment, registration, equipment prep, and payment setup, you’re ready to go. We’re glad you’re here — welcome to the ROW family.

Parent’s Code of Conduct

If children are to grow and develop in their sport, an environment of positive communication and respect must exist. The following code is adapted from a resource manual developed for community coaches by the Canadian Centre for Ethics in Sport (CCES).

  • I will remember that my child plays sport for their enjoyment, not for mine.
  • I will encourage my child to play by the rules and to resolve conflicts without hostility or violence.
  • I will teach my child that doing one’s best is as important as winning, so they never feel defeated by the outcome of a meet.
  • I will make my child feel like a winner every time by offering praise for competing fairly and trying hard.
  • I will never ridicule or yell at my child for making a mistake or losing a race.
  • I will remember that children learn best by example, and will applaud good performances by both my child’s team and their opponents.
  • I will not force my child to participate in sports.
  • I will never question an official’s judgment or honesty in public.
  • I will support all efforts to remove verbal and physical abuse from children’s sporting activities.
  • I will respect and show appreciation for the trained coaches who give their time to my child’s development.

Financial Assistance

ROW partners with municipal and community programs, and also offers its own bursary, to help make swimming accessible to families who need support with the cost of the program.

City of Waterloo Fee Assistance

The City of Waterloo Fee Assistance Leisure Access Program provides financial support, in the form of a credit, to permanent residents with low income living in Waterloo, to help them participate in City of Waterloo recreational and affiliated minor sport and arts programs. Eligibility is based on total family income — see the City of Waterloo’s program page for details.

KidSport Kitchener-Waterloo Bursary

ROW partners with KidSport Kitchener Waterloo to help ensure children residing in the Kitchener-Waterloo area can participate in organized swimming. Contact the ROW office for details on applying.

ROW Training Bursary

Members sponsored by either of the programs above may additionally apply for ROW’s own training bursary. This club-sponsored program is open to all members, occurs after registration, and has an annual maximum grant limit reviewed yearly by the ROW Board of Directors. Any bursary awarded is applied as a credit to the family account.

  • Application period: November 1–15 each year, with results communicated by December 1. Applications continue to be accepted afterward and reviewed if funds remain available that season.
  • How to apply: email the application to office@rowswimming.ca, or drop it off in the ROW mailbox at WLU in a sealed envelope marked “Training Bursary Application.” Applications are reviewed by an independent committee.

Fundraising & Sponsorship

ROW’s two principal club fundraisers are the fall Raffle and the spring Swim-a-thon. We also use FlipGive and welcome corporate Sponsorship throughout the year.

Raffle Fundraiser

ROW implements a recoverable fundraising fee based on your swimmer’s group. Currently, your fundraising commitment goes towards the purchase of raffle tickets — for each $100 paid, you receive a book of tickets, which you can sell or keep for a chance at one of the prizes. It’s your responsibility to return ticket stubs to ROW before the December draw date, which is typically held at the club’s Annual General Meeting.

Questions? Contact fundraising@rowswimming.ca.

Swim-a-thon

Every year, ROW participates in the Swim Ontario Swim-a-Thon. Details for the current season are shared when available. Raising money through the Swim-a-thon also counts towards your family’s Family Partnership Program points — see the FPP Guide for point values.

Sponsorship

ROW welcomes corporate sponsorship — it helps enhance facilities, provide training resources, and offer financial assistance to athletes in need, while giving sponsors exposure within our network of swimmers, families, and fans.

Full sponsorship levels and details are on our How to Get Involved page.

Parent FAQ

Answers to the questions that come up most once you’re settled in — practices, coaches, meets, billing, and volunteering.

Practices

What happens if I miss a practice?
Unfortunately, there’s no refund for missed practices. As a courtesy, notify your coach of any known absences in advance — vacations, illness, family commitments.
When should we arrive, and what should my swimmer bring to practice?
Arrive with enough time for your swimmer to be ready on deck 15 minutes before practice starts — this time is used for activation and warm-up. Every swimmer needs a black training suit, ROW swim cap, and goggles. Other equipment (fins, paddles, snorkels) is group-specific; check your group’s page under the Programs tab for the full equipment list.
Do I need to stay and watch practice?
No. If you leave, please pick your child up on time. If you stay, you’re welcome to view from the gallery above the pool deck — please avoid coaching from the gallery, giving hand signals, or otherwise distracting the group.

Coaches & Communication

How do I contact my swimmer’s coach?
Email your coach directly — all coach emails are listed under the Coaches tab on the website. Please allow up to 48 hours for a response; coaches won’t reply on weekends or during meets.
If a conflict arises, how do I seek resolution?
Contact Swim Ontario through their Club Resources page for help filing a complaint. For more on the process, see Swim Ontario’s Sport Safety policies, including Complaints, Dispute Resolution, Discipline, and Appeals procedures.

Meets & Other Events

Why should my swimmer attend meets?
Meets are where training becomes experience — your swimmer races against the clock, competes alongside peers from other clubs, and gets a feel for what the sport looks and feels like at game time. Even swimmers not aiming for elite levels benefit from attending a few meets each year.
Are all meets equally competitive?
No. Most are sanctioned by Swim Ontario or Swimming Canada. Others, like ROW’s fall “Black and White” event or informal “Series” meets, are more relaxed and aimed at newer or younger swimmers.
What are qualifying time standards?
Some higher-level meets require a previously achieved time to enter. Standards vary by event, age, and gender, and are published on Swim Ontario’s site or in individual Meet Packages.
How many meets should we attend?
Your coach will guide this. As a rough rule, one meet every four to six weeks is common at the lower levels — more isn’t always better since frequent meets cut into training time.
Short course vs. long course, what’s the difference?
Short course (SC) meets are held in 25m pools, mostly in the first half of the season. Long course (LC) meets are in 50m pools, from February onward. Times are tracked separately.
How long is a meet, and which session does my swimmer swim in?
Meets range from one day to three or four days at higher levels, typically divided into sessions of roughly 4–5 hours (a one-hour warm-up followed by races). Which session your swimmer is in is usually set by age — younger swimmers (12 & under) typically swim in the afternoon, older swimmers (13+) in the morning and sometimes again in the evening for finals, though this can vary by meet. Your coach chooses 2–3 events per day. You’re welcome to stay and cheer the whole time, but you may leave once your swimmer has been excused from the deck by their coach.
Where do I find the meet schedule?
Meets/Events → Meet Schedule on the club website for a long-range overview, or Meets/Events again for detailed info and Accept/Decline buttons for meets coming up in the next month or two.
What does Accepting or Declining a meet mean?
If you don’t explicitly decline a meet before the RSVP deadline, your swimmer will be entered automatically and you’ll be responsible for the applicable fees — there’s no default to your coach’s discretion. Only accounts in good standing are eligible to be entered in meets. To respond, go to Meets/Events on the club website, find your meet, and use the Attend/Decline button; there’s a notes field if your swimmer can only attend part of the meet.
What’s a Meet Package?
A document from the host club with the full schedule, days, times, and sessions for every event. Pair it with the Meet Entries list to know exactly when your swimmer needs to be there.
How does event entry work?
Your coach chooses which events your swimmer races. Coaches typically submit preliminary entries and share them with families to double-check before a set deadline — changes usually can’t be made after that point, so confirm promptly. If your swimmer wants to race a specific event, have them raise it with their coach directly.
What should we pack?
A race-legal black swimsuit (practice suit or plain black/blue clothing is fine at lower levels, or before your ROW gear arrives), 2 ROW swim caps, and 2 pairs of goggles. For on deck between races, pack warm layers — a long-sleeve shirt, t-shirt, pants or shorts, and deck shoes. For snacks, healthier options fuel better racing: fruit, granola or protein bars, trail mix, cheese and crackers, or sandwiches, plus plenty of water (electrolyte drinks are fine too). Fins and hand paddles aren’t allowed during warm-ups or races.
What am I committing to once my swimmer is entered in a meet?
The full session — from warm-up through all of your swimmer’s races, including heats and finals, and any relay they’re placed on. If there’s a specific reason your swimmer needs to leave early or miss part of a session, let your coach know in advance (ideally during RSVP) rather than the day of the meet.
Will I need to volunteer at meets?
Likely yes. At ROW-hosted meets your swimmer is entered in, expect to be assigned an officiating role like Timer, usually notified a week or two ahead. No experience needed — see our Officiating page for the full list of volunteer roles and how training works.
What is “scratching,” and why does it matter?
If your swimmer can’t attend, tell your coach as soon as possible so they can withdraw (“scratch”) them before heats are finalized. Not doing so leaves an empty lane, unfair to other swimmers, and can result in fines at higher-level meets.
How early should we arrive?
Aim to have your swimmer on deck 20–30 minutes before their scheduled warm-up — this time is used to go over meet logistics and run through an activation routine. Confirm the exact time with your coach.

Invoices, Fees & Payments

Will I get my invoices by email?
No. ROW generates a monthly invoice for each family, but it isn’t emailed to you. You’ll need to sign in to the club website to view it.
How do I find my invoices and billing history?
Sign in at rowswimming.ca, go to My Account → $ My Invoice/Payment, then select Billing History. Your most recent invoice appears first.
How do I reconcile my invoice across several months?
Under Billing History, use Search Billing/Payment History and enter a date range. It totals your fees and payments for that period. Note: the totals may look off if your balance wasn’t $0 at the start of the range.
What fees should I expect to see?
Registration Fee — one-time, non-refundable, includes Swim Ontario membership (required for sanctioned meets, and provides insurance).
Training Fee — varies by group, since higher-level groups train more and longer.
Meet Fees — charged by the host club for each meet you’re entered in.
Team Travel Fee — covers coaching costs at meets; $0 for ROW-hosted meets, typically around $25/swimmer for others.
If I’m paying the same amount each month, why isn’t the number of practices always the same?
Monthly payments are a service to members who wish to split up their annual training and swim meet fees into smaller installments — these payments do not represent a monthly membership fee. The actual number of practices can vary month to month (holidays, school breaks, and meet weekends all shift the count) while your payment stays level.
When do I owe Meet Fees?
Whenever your swimmer is entered in a meet, whether you Accepted it yourself or your coach entered them by default. Fees are owed even if your swimmer ends up not attending.
How do Pre-Authorized Debits (PADs) handle meet fees?
Your monthly PAD amount already factors in your swimmer’s expected meet participation. If your actual meet fees run higher, your final month’s PAD is adjusted up. If lower, the credit carries over to next season, or can be refunded on request.
How can I pay?
Pre-Authorized Debit (PAD) — complete and return the Payor’s Pre-Authorized Debit (AFT) Agreement with a void cheque at registration.
Cheque — payable to ROW Swim Club, dropped in the ROW mailbox near the WLU pool viewing gallery, or mailed to ROW Swim Club, c/o Wilfrid Laurier University Athletic Complex, 75 University Ave W, Waterloo, ON N2L 3C5.
Credit card — accepted through TeamUnify.
Who do I contact with billing questions?
Our Office Manager at office@rowswimming.ca.

Volunteering & FPP

A great swim club isn’t built from the sidelines; it’s built together.

Is there a parent volunteer requirement? Yes, for most families — ROW is a volunteer-based, not-for-profit club. A points system (the Family Partnership Program, or FPP) tracks this, with different roles worth different point values. First-year families may qualify for an exemption. See the FPP Guide for point targets by group, exemption details, and the full list of ways to earn points.
