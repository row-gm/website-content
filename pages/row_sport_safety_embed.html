Sport Safety

How to report a concern, what Rowan’s Law requires, and where to find support.

A shared commitment

Safe sport at ROW is not a standalone club policy. It is the local link in a chain that runs from the federal government through our national and provincial bodies, down to every practice and meet we run.

WhoWhat they do
WhoGovernment of CanadaWhat they doSets the national mandate through the Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport, and funds the independent Canadian Safe Sport Program.
WhoSwimming CanadaWhat they doHas adopted the Canadian Safe Sport Program nationally, and states plainly that every athlete, coach, official and volunteer has the right to a training and competitive environment free of abuse, harassment or discrimination.
WhoSwim OntarioWhat they doCarries that mandate provincially, committed to fun, healthy, inclusive and safe environments built on fairness, integrity, open communication and mutual respect.
WhoROW Swim ClubWhat they doWe are the local stakeholders. We create the day to day environments aligned with that commitment, and we protect our members from anything that is not.

Reporting a concern

If something is wrong, raise it. Start with your swimmer’s coach before or after a practice, then the Head Coach, then the Member Liaison at memberliaison@rowswimming.ca.

For help filing a formal complaint, contact Swim Ontario through their Club Resources page, which also holds the procedures for complaints, dispute resolution, discipline and appeals.

Rowan’s Law

Ontario’s Rowan’s Law concussion regulations came into effect on 1 July 2019 and are mandatory for every sports organization.

As part of registration, all swimmers and families under 26, and all coaches and officials at Level 3 and above, must confirm they have read and agreed to the Rowan’s Law resources and declaration.

More is on the Government of Ontario site and on Swim Ontario’s Concussion Safety page.
