What to Expect at Meets

The shape of a racing season, the patches that worry people, and what genuinely helps.

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

Not every meet is a big meet

A season has more meets than Peak meets, and they are not all for the same thing. Every meet on the schedule carries a tag that tells you what it is for. Knowing which is which explains a lot of what you see on the day.

Tag What it means What to expect on the day
TagPeak What it meansThe top of your racing calendar. This is the biggest meet of your season. What to expect on the dayTraining drops right down beforehand, so your swimmer arrives fresh and fast. Your coach names these at the start of the season.
TagPerformance What it meansYou prepare for this one, and you race it chasing a personal best. What to expect on the dayTraining eases a little in the days before. Best times are the point here.
TagPathway Skills What it meansYou put what you have been working on in training to the test. What to expect on the dayRaced in the middle of hard training, on purpose. Your swimmer will be tired. These are for practising pacing, starts and race plans, not for chasing a best time.
TagTeam What it meansYou race for the team, and you are there for the swimmers beside you. What to expect on the dayTraining carries on as normal that week. Judge the weekend on how they raced and how they backed up their teammates.
A swimmer can race well without a taper. If your swimmer is racing tired at a Pathway Skills meet, that is the plan. Judge that weekend on how they swam it, not on the clock.

Flat patches are part of it

Almost every swimmer has stretches with no best times. Some of that is training phase. Some of it is growth. Very little of it means anything is wrong.

Two things sit behind most of it. A swimmer’s growth spurt changes their swimming for a while, and it arrives at very different ages. And because age groups run January to December, two swimmers in one group can be almost a year apart. Both are covered in this section.

Every season adds a layer to the foundation. A season with few best times is not a wasted one, and this year and the long run are not in competition.

What actually helps

Four things, and none of them happen at the pool.

  Why it matters
Sleep Why it mattersThe single biggest one, and it costs nothing. Growing bodies training hard need more sleep than they think.
Food Why it mattersRegular meals, and something to eat soon after practice. A swimmer who trains hungry does not adapt to the training.
Turning up Why it mattersConsistency beats intensity at every age. A swimmer who trains most weeks for years beats one who trains brilliantly in bursts.
What you say after a race Why it mattersAsk what went well and what they would change. Those two questions do more than any amount of analysis, and they hand the thinking back to the swimmer, which is the point.

How to ask about your swimmer

You will get two proper conversations a year with your swimmer's coach, about the objectives set for that phase. Those are the ones for the bigger questions.

In between, catch your swimmer’s coach before or after a practice. What works less well is a conversation about training in front of your swimmer, because it puts them in the middle of it.

Coaches are working from a written plan for the group, and are happy to explain what their swimmer is working on and why. That is a different conversation from one about the plan itself, and it is usually the more useful one.
